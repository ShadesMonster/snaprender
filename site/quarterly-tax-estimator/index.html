Understanding Quarterly Estimated Taxes

If you're a freelancer, independent contractor, or self-employed individual in the US, you're generally required to make quarterly estimated tax payments to the IRS. Unlike W-2 employees who have taxes withheld from each paycheck, self-employed workers must pay taxes proactively throughout the year.

Who Needs to Pay Quarterly Taxes?

You generally need to make estimated tax payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 in tax for the year after subtracting withholding and credits. This applies to freelancers, sole proprietors, partners, S-corporation shareholders, and anyone with significant income not subject to withholding.

The Four Quarterly Deadlines

  • Q1 (Jan 1 – Mar 31): Payment due April 15
  • Q2 (Apr 1 – May 31): Payment due June 15
  • Q3 (Jun 1 – Aug 31): Payment due September 15
  • Q4 (Sep 1 – Dec 31): Payment due January 15 of the following year

Self-Employment Tax Explained

Self-employment tax covers Social Security (12.4%) and Medicare (2.9%) taxes. As a self-employed person, you pay both the employer and employee portions, totaling 15.3%. This is calculated on 92.35% of your net self-employment income. The Social Security portion has a wage base cap ($168,600 for 2024), but Medicare has no cap.

Safe Harbor Rule

To avoid underpayment penalties, you should pay at least the lesser of: (1) 90% of your current year's tax liability, or (2) 100% of your prior year's tax liability (110% if your AGI exceeded $150,000). This is known as the "safe harbor" rule and is a common strategy for freelancers with variable income.

Tips for Managing Quarterly Taxes

  • Set aside money immediately: When you receive a payment, transfer your tax percentage to a separate savings account.
  • Track expenses diligently: Business expenses reduce your taxable income. Keep receipts and records for everything.
  • Consider a SEP IRA or Solo 401(k): Retirement contributions can significantly reduce your taxable income.
  • Use IRS Direct Pay or EFTPS: Make payments electronically to get instant confirmation.
  • Adjust quarterly: If your income changes significantly, recalculate and adjust your estimated payments.
