How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate

Setting the right hourly rate is the difference between a thriving freelance career and burning out for less than minimum wage. Most freelancers make the mistake of picking a number that "feels right" instead of calculating what they actually need to earn.

This calculator works backwards from your goals: what do you want to take home, what are your real costs, and how many hours can you actually bill? The formula is straightforward:

Hourly Rate = (Desired Income + Expenses) / (1 - Tax Rate) / Billable Hours Per Year

Why Your Freelance Rate Should Be Higher Than You Think

Full-time employees get benefits that freelancers have to pay for out of pocket:

  • Health insurance: $6,000-$15,000/year that comes out of your rate
  • Self-employment tax: An extra 15.3% on top of income tax (in the US)
  • No paid time off: Every vacation day, sick day, and holiday is unpaid
  • Non-billable time: Invoicing, marketing, admin, proposals - typically 20-40% of your work hours
  • No employer 401k match: You fund your own retirement
  • Equipment and software: Your laptop, tools, and subscriptions are on you

A common rule of thumb: a freelancer needs to charge roughly 2-3x the equivalent hourly wage of a full-time employee to end up with the same take-home pay and benefits.

Freelance Rate by Experience Level

While rates vary wildly by industry and specialization, here are rough benchmarks for US-based freelancers:

  • Beginner (0-2 years): $25-$75/hour
  • Intermediate (2-5 years): $50-$125/hour
  • Expert (5-10 years): $100-$200/hour
  • Specialist/Consultant (10+ years): $150-$400+/hour

These ranges vary significantly by field. A freelance developer or designer will typically command higher rates than a freelance writer, but a specialized technical writer or UX researcher may out-earn a generalist developer.

The Billable Hours Trap

The biggest mistake new freelancers make is assuming they can bill 40 hours a week. In reality, a significant portion of your time goes to:

  • Finding and pitching new clients
  • Writing proposals and scoping projects
  • Administrative work (invoicing, bookkeeping, emails)
  • Marketing and maintaining your online presence
  • Learning and skill development

Most established freelancers bill 60-70% of their working hours. New freelancers often bill only 40-50%. This calculator accounts for this with the "billable percentage" input - be honest with yourself about how much time you actually spend on paid client work.

Should You Charge Hourly or Project-Based?

This calculator gives you an hourly rate, but many freelancers earn more by charging per project. Here's when to use each:

  • Hourly: Best for ongoing work, maintenance, consulting calls, and when scope is unclear
  • Project-based: Best when you can estimate scope accurately and want to be rewarded for efficiency
  • Value-based: Best for high-impact work where your deliverable has measurable business value (e.g., a landing page that generates leads)

Even if you charge by the project, knowing your minimum hourly rate helps you estimate project prices. Divide your project quote by estimated hours to make sure you're not accidentally working below your floor rate.
