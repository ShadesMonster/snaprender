How French Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

In France, take-home pay (salaire net après impôt) is what remains of your gross salary (salaire brut) after two main categories of deductions: social contributions (cotisations sociales) and income tax (impôt sur le revenu, or IR). Since January 2019, income tax is withheld at source (prélèvement à la source), so most employees see both deductions on their payslip (fiche de paie).

Social Contributions (Cotisations Sociales)

French employee social contributions fund the country's comprehensive social security system, covering healthcare (Assurance Maladie), retirement pensions (retraite de base and complémentaire AGIRC-ARRCO), unemployment insurance, and family benefits. As an employee, you typically pay around 22% of your gross salary in social charges. Key components include:

  • CSG (Contribution Sociale Généralisée): 9.2% on 98.25% of gross salary. Of this, 6.8% is deductible from taxable income and 2.4% is non-deductible.
  • CRDS (Contribution au Remboursement de la Dette Sociale): 0.5% on 98.25% of gross salary, non-deductible.
  • Pension (retraite de base): 6.9% on earnings up to the Social Security ceiling (Plafond de la Sécurité Sociale, €46,368/year in 2024).
  • Complementary pension (AGIRC-ARRCO): 3.15% on Tranche 1 (up to €46,368) and 8.64% on Tranche 2 (from €46,368 to €370,944).
  • Unemployment insurance: 0% for employees since October 2018 (fully employer-funded).
  • Health insurance (Assurance Maladie): 0% employee share (employer-paid in mainland France).

For simplicity, this calculator uses a combined employee social contribution rate of approximately 22%, which is the standard approximation for cadre and non-cadre employees in the private sector.

French Income Tax (Impôt sur le Revenu)

France uses a progressive income tax system with five brackets (tranches) applied to your net taxable income per fiscal part (quotient familial). The 2024 brackets are:

  • €0 – €11,294: 0%
  • €11,295 – €28,797: 11%
  • €28,798 – €82,341: 30%
  • €82,342 – €177,106: 41%
  • Above €177,106: 45%

Quotient Familial (Family Quotient System)

France's distinctive quotient familial system divides your taxable income by the number of "parts" in your household before applying tax brackets, then multiplies the resulting tax back by the same number of parts. This mechanism provides meaningful tax savings for families with children.

  • Single (célibataire): 1 part
  • Married or PACS: 2 parts
  • 1st and 2nd child: +0.5 parts each
  • 3rd child onward: +1 part each

For example, a married couple with 3 children has 2 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 1 = 4 parts. Their income is divided by 4 for bracket application, then the tax is multiplied by 4.

Salaire Net vs Salaire Brut

In France, job offers typically quote the salaire brut (gross salary). To convert to salaire net avant impôt (net salary before tax), you subtract employee social contributions (roughly 22%). To get the salaire net après impôt (net salary after tax), you also subtract income tax withheld at source. This calculator computes both steps for you.

The 10% Professional Expenses Deduction

French tax law automatically applies a 10% deduction on your net taxable salary to account for professional expenses (frais professionnels), unless you opt to deduct actual expenses (frais réels). This calculator applies the standard 10% deduction. The deduction is capped at €14,171 and has a minimum floor of €495 for 2024.

Tips to Optimize Your French Take-Home Pay

  • Maximize your quotient familial: Marriage or PACS can significantly reduce taxes for couples with unequal incomes.
  • Consider frais réels: If your actual professional expenses exceed 10% of salary (long commute, home office costs), opt for actual deduction.
  • Use PER (Plan d'Épargne Retraite): Contributions to a PER are deductible from taxable income, reducing your income tax.
  • Check your tax rate: The prélèvement à la source rate can be adjusted if your situation changes (marriage, children, salary change).
  • Understand your fiche de paie: Your payslip shows both the salaire net (after social charges) and salaire net fiscal (taxable net), which differ because non-deductible CSG/CRDS are added back.
