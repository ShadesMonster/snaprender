How New Zealand Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

Your take-home pay is what remains of your gross salary after PAYE income tax, ACC earners' levy, KiwiSaver contributions, and student loan repayments are deducted. New Zealand uses a Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system where your employer deducts these amounts from each pay before you receive it. Unlike many other countries, New Zealand has no tax-free threshold — you pay tax on every dollar you earn from the first dollar.

New Zealand PAYE Income Tax (2024–25)

New Zealand uses a progressive income tax system with five brackets. The rates apply uniformly nationwide — there are no regional or state taxes. The brackets are: 10.5% on income up to $14,000; 17.5% on income from $14,001 to $48,000; 30% on income from $48,001 to $70,000; 33% on income from $70,001 to $180,000; and 39% on income above $180,000. There is no personal allowance or standard deduction, so your entire gross salary is subject to tax.

ACC Earners' Levy

The Accident Compensation Corporation (ACC) earners' levy is a compulsory levy that funds New Zealand's no-fault accident insurance scheme. For the 2024–25 tax year, the rate is 1.60% of your liable earnings, up to a maximum of $142,283 in earnings. This levy is deducted from your pay by your employer through the PAYE system. ACC covers all residents and visitors for personal injury from accidents, regardless of fault, and provides treatment costs, income compensation, and rehabilitation support.

KiwiSaver

KiwiSaver is New Zealand's voluntary, work-based savings initiative designed to help you save for retirement. If you are enrolled, you can choose an employee contribution rate of 3%, 4%, 6%, 8%, or 10% of your gross salary. Your employer is required to contribute at least 3% on top of your salary (though employer contributions are subject to ESCT). KiwiSaver contributions are deducted from your after-tax pay, meaning they do not reduce your taxable income. You can apply for a contributions holiday if you need to temporarily stop contributing. New employees are automatically enrolled but can opt out within a limited window.

Student Loan Repayment

If you have a student loan from StudyLink, repayments are deducted automatically through the PAYE system once your income exceeds the repayment threshold. For the 2024–25 tax year, the repayment rate is 12% on every dollar you earn above $22,828 per year. For example, if you earn $72,000, you would repay 12% of ($72,000 − $22,828) = $5,900.64 per year. Student loan repayments are deducted after tax has been calculated — they reduce your take-home pay but not your tax liability.

Tips to Increase Your Take-Home Pay

  • Review your KiwiSaver rate: While saving for retirement is important, choosing a lower contribution rate (3% vs 10%) will increase your immediate take-home pay. Consider your long-term goals before reducing contributions.
  • Check your tax code: Make sure your employer is using the correct PAYE tax code (e.g., M for primary employment, S if you have a student loan). An incorrect code can lead to over- or under-deductions.
  • Claim tax credits: If you are eligible for the Independent Earner Tax Credit (IETC) for income between $24,000 and $48,000, you may be able to claim up to $520 per year.
  • Voluntary student loan repayments: Making extra repayments above the compulsory amount can reduce your loan balance faster and eliminate the deduction sooner, increasing future take-home pay.
  • Use a PIE fund for savings: Portfolio Investment Entity (PIE) funds cap your tax rate at 28%, which may be lower than your marginal PAYE rate if you earn over $48,000.
