How UK Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

Your take-home pay is what remains of your gross salary after income tax, National Insurance contributions (NICs), student loan repayments, and pension contributions are deducted. The UK uses a Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system where your employer deducts these amounts from your pay before you receive it.

UK Income Tax (2024/25)

The UK uses a progressive income tax system. Everyone receives a tax-free Personal Allowance of £12,570 per year. Income above this threshold is taxed in bands: the basic rate of 20% applies to income from £12,571 to £50,270, the higher rate of 40% applies from £50,271 to £125,140, and the additional rate of 45% applies to income above £125,140. For those earning over £100,000, the Personal Allowance tapers away at a rate of £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000, meaning it is fully eliminated at £125,140.

Scottish Income Tax

If you live in Scotland, different income tax rates and bands apply. Scotland has six tax bands: a starter rate of 19% (£12,571–£14,876), basic rate of 20% (£14,877–£26,561), intermediate rate of 21% (£26,562–£43,662), higher rate of 42% (£43,663–£75,000), advanced rate of 45% (£75,001–£125,140), and a top rate of 48% on income above £125,140. The same Personal Allowance of £12,570 applies, and the same tapering rules for income above £100,000.

National Insurance Contributions (NICs)

Employee Class 1 National Insurance is charged at 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 per year, and 2% on earnings above £50,270. NI is calculated on your gross earnings (before pension deductions if using a net pay arrangement) and funds the state pension, NHS, and other benefits. Unlike income tax, National Insurance does not have a personal allowance that tapers — the thresholds are fixed.

Student Loan Repayments

If you have a student loan, repayments are deducted automatically through PAYE. The amount depends on your plan type: Plan 1 (pre-2012 England/Wales, or Northern Ireland/Scotland) charges 9% on income above £22,015; Plan 2 (post-2012 England/Wales) charges 9% on income above £27,295; Plan 4 (Scotland post-1998) charges 9% above £27,660; and the Postgraduate Loan charges 6% above £21,000. If you have both an undergraduate and postgraduate loan, both are deducted simultaneously.

Pension Tax Relief

Contributions to a workplace pension reduce your taxable income, which means you pay less income tax. Most workplace pensions operate on a “relief at source” or “net pay” basis. This calculator assumes a net pay arrangement where your pension contribution is deducted from your gross salary before income tax is calculated. Pension contributions also reduce the income used to calculate student loan repayments. The annual allowance for pension contributions is £60,000 for most people in 2024/25.

Tips to Increase Your Take-Home Pay

  • Salary sacrifice for pension: By sacrificing salary into your pension, you save both income tax and National Insurance, increasing the overall value of your pension contributions.
  • Check your tax code: An incorrect tax code can mean you overpay or underpay tax. Check your payslip and contact HMRC if it looks wrong.
  • Claim Marriage Allowance: If your partner earns less than £12,570 and you are a basic rate taxpayer, you can transfer £1,260 of their allowance to reduce your tax by up to £252 per year.
  • Use your ISA allowance: While not a salary deduction, the £20,000 annual ISA allowance lets you earn investment income and gains tax-free.
  • Childcare vouchers or Tax-Free Childcare: If you have children, government schemes can reduce your childcare costs, effectively increasing your disposable income.
