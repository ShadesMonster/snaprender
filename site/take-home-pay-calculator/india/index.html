How In-Hand Salary Is Calculated in India

Your in-hand salary (also called take-home pay or net salary) is what remains after all deductions from your Cost to Company (CTC) or gross salary. The main deductions in India are income tax, health and education cess, Employee Provident Fund (EPF), and professional tax. The amount you pay depends heavily on which tax regime you choose and the deductions you can claim.

New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023-24)

The New Tax Regime was introduced to simplify the tax structure. From FY 2023-24 (AY 2024-25), it became the default regime for all taxpayers. It features six tax slabs with lower rates but does not allow most deductions and exemptions available under the old regime. The new regime includes a standard deduction of ₹75,000 (raised from ₹50,000 in Budget 2024). Under the new regime, if your total income is ₹7,00,000 or less after the standard deduction, you qualify for a full tax rebate under Section 87A, effectively making your tax liability zero.

  • ₹0 to ₹3,00,000: Nil (0%)
  • ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000: 5%
  • ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 10%
  • ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
  • ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%
  • ₹15,00,001 and above: 30%

Old Tax Regime

The Old Tax Regime has higher tax rates but allows numerous deductions and exemptions. Taxpayers can opt for it if they have significant investments and expenses that qualify for deductions. It offers a standard deduction of ₹50,000 plus additional deductions under various sections:

  • ₹0 to ₹2,50,000: Nil (0%)
  • ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5%
  • ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%
  • ₹10,00,001 and above: 30%

Section 80C Deductions

Section 80C is the most widely used deduction under the old regime. It allows deductions up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year for investments and expenses including EPF contributions, PPF (Public Provident Fund), ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premiums, NSC (National Savings Certificate), 5-year fixed deposits, tuition fees for children, and home loan principal repayment. Most salaried individuals in India can easily exhaust this limit through a combination of EPF and other investments.

Section 80D — Health Insurance

Under Section 80D, you can claim deductions for health insurance premiums. Individuals below 60 years can claim up to ₹25,000 for self and family. An additional ₹25,000 (or ₹50,000 for senior citizen parents) can be claimed for parents' health insurance. This deduction is available only under the old tax regime.

Health & Education Cess

A 4% Health and Education Cess is levied on the total income tax payable (including surcharge, if any). This cess applies under both the new and old tax regimes. It funds primary education and healthcare services. The cess is calculated after all deductions and rebates have been applied to your base tax.

Employee Provident Fund (EPF)

EPF is a mandatory retirement savings scheme for salaried employees in India. Both the employee and employer contribute 12% of the basic salary. Basic salary is typically 40-50% of CTC. The contribution is capped at a basic salary of ₹15,000 per month, meaning the maximum employee contribution is ₹1,800 per month or ₹21,600 per year. Many companies allow voluntary contributions above this cap. EPF contributions by the employee are eligible for deduction under Section 80C in the old regime.

Professional Tax

Professional tax is a state-level tax levied on salaried individuals and professionals. The rate and slab structure varies by state but is capped at ₹2,500 per year by law. Not all states levy professional tax. States like Maharashtra, Karnataka, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana charge professional tax, while states like Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan, and Uttarakhand do not. Professional tax is deductible from taxable income under both regimes.

Tips to Reduce Your Tax Liability

  • Compare both regimes: Use this calculator with both New and Old regime to see which saves you more tax. The old regime is often better if you have significant 80C, 80D, and HRA deductions.
  • Maximize Section 80C: Invest the full ₹1,50,000 in EPF, PPF, ELSS, or other eligible instruments under the old regime.
  • Claim HRA exemption: If you live in rented accommodation, claim House Rent Allowance under the old regime for significant tax savings.
  • Get health insurance: A ₹25,000 health insurance premium deduction under 80D reduces your taxable income and provides financial protection.
  • NPS contribution: Additional ₹50,000 deduction under Section 80CCD(1B) for National Pension System contributions (available in the old regime and partially in the new regime).
  • Home loan benefits: Interest up to ₹2,00,000 under Section 24(b) and principal under 80C in the old regime.
