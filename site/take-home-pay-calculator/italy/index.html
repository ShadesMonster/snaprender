How Italian Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

In Italy, your take-home pay (stipendio netto) is what remains of your gross annual salary (RAL — Retribuzione Annua Lorda) after mandatory deductions. The main deductions are IRPEF income tax, INPS social security contributions, and regional and municipal surcharges. Unlike many countries, Italian employees typically receive their salary spread across 13 or 14 monthly payments (mensilità), which affects the amount of each individual paycheck but not the annual total.

IRPEF — Imposta sul Reddito delle Persone Fisiche

IRPEF is Italy's progressive income tax. For 2024, there are three tax brackets:

  • €0 – €28,000: 23%
  • €28,001 – €50,000: 35%
  • €50,001 and above: 43%

IRPEF is calculated on your taxable income, which is your gross salary minus INPS employee contributions. The tax is progressive, meaning you only pay the higher rate on the portion of income that falls within each bracket, not on your entire salary.

Detrazioni per Lavoro Dipendente (Employment Tax Credits)

Italian employees are entitled to tax deductions (detrazioni) that reduce the amount of IRPEF owed. These credits are designed to provide tax relief, particularly for lower and middle-income earners. The deduction amount decreases as income rises:

  • Income up to €15,000: €1,955 deduction
  • €15,001 – €28,000: deduction decreases proportionally from €1,910
  • €28,001 – €50,000: deduction decreases proportionally from €1,910
  • Above €50,000: no deduction

This system effectively creates a no-tax area (no-tax area) of approximately €8,174 for employees, because the detrazioni offset the IRPEF that would be due on that amount of income.

INPS Social Security Contributions

INPS (Istituto Nazionale della Previdenza Sociale) is Italy's national social security system. Employees contribute a mandatory percentage of their gross salary. The standard employee rate is 9.19% on earnings up to the annual ceiling of €55,008. For income above this ceiling, the rate increases to 10.19%. These contributions fund pensions, unemployment insurance, and other social welfare programs. Your employer also pays a separate, larger contribution on top of your salary.

Regional and Municipal Surcharges

In addition to national IRPEF, Italian residents pay addizionale regionale (regional surcharge) and addizionale comunale (municipal surcharge). These vary significantly by location:

  • Regional surcharge: Ranges from approximately 1.23% to 3.33% depending on the region. For example, Lazio has higher rates than many other regions.
  • Municipal surcharge: Typically around 0.8% on average, but varies by municipality, with some cities charging up to 0.9%.

This calculator uses region-specific rates for the regional surcharge and a 0.8% average for the municipal surcharge.

Mensilità — 13th and 14th Month Pay

Italian employment contracts typically include a tredicesima (13th month payment), paid in December as a kind of Christmas bonus. Many contracts, especially in certain sectors like commerce, also include a quattordicesima (14th month payment), usually paid in June or July. Your RAL is divided by the number of mensilità to determine your monthly gross paycheck. For example, with a €38,000 RAL and 13 mensilità, each monthly gross payment is approximately €2,923.

Tips to Understand Your Busta Paga

  • RAL vs. Net: Your RAL (Retribuzione Annua Lorda) is the gross annual figure in your contract. Your net monthly pay will be significantly lower after IRPEF, INPS, and surcharges.
  • TFR (Trattamento di Fine Rapporto): Italian employers set aside approximately 6.9% of your salary as severance pay (TFR). This is not deducted from your paycheck — it is an additional cost to the employer and paid out when you leave.
  • Tax deductions for dependents: If you have dependent family members (spouse, children), you may be entitled to additional detrazioni that further reduce your IRPEF.
  • Bonus €100 (ex-Renzi bonus): Workers with income between certain thresholds may receive a monthly bonus of up to approximately €100, now integrated into the detrazioni system.
  • Compare regions: Moving to a region with lower addizionale regionale can save you several hundred euros per year.
