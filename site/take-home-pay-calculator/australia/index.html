How Take-Home Pay Is Calculated in Australia

Your take-home pay in Australia is your gross salary minus income tax, the Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge, and any compulsory HECS/HELP repayments. Unlike the US, Australia has no state or territory income taxes — all income tax is levied at the federal level by the Australian Taxation Office (ATO). There is also no separate Social Security or payroll tax deducted from employees.

Australian Income Tax (2024–25 Resident Rates)

Australia uses a progressive income tax system. For the 2024–25 financial year (1 July 2024 to 30 June 2025), the individual resident tax rates are:

  • $0 – $18,200: Nil (tax-free threshold)
  • $18,201 – $45,000: 16 cents for each $1 over $18,200
  • $45,001 – $135,000: $4,288 plus 30 cents for each $1 over $45,000
  • $135,001 – $190,000: $31,288 plus 37 cents for each $1 over $135,000
  • $190,001 and over: $51,638 plus 45 cents for each $1 over $190,000

The tax-free threshold of $18,200 means that Australians earning below this amount pay no income tax at all. Tax is calculated progressively — you only pay the higher rate on the portion of income within each bracket, not on your entire salary.

Medicare Levy

The Medicare levy is a flat 2% of your taxable income, which funds Australia's public healthcare system (Medicare). Most Australian taxpayers pay this levy. Low-income earners may receive a reduction or exemption. The Medicare levy is separate from private health insurance — even if you have private cover, you still pay the 2% levy.

Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)

If you earn above $93,000 (singles) or $186,000 (families) and do not hold an appropriate level of private patient hospital cover, you must pay the Medicare levy surcharge in addition to the standard 2% Medicare levy. The surcharge rates for 2024–25 are:

  • $93,001 – $108,000: 1.0%
  • $108,001 – $144,000: 1.25%
  • $144,001 and over: 1.5%

The MLS is designed to encourage higher-income earners to take out private health insurance and reduce demand on the public system.

HECS/HELP Repayments

If you have an outstanding Higher Education Loan Program (HELP) debt — formerly known as HECS — compulsory repayments are deducted through the tax system once your repayment income exceeds the minimum threshold. For 2024–25, the repayment thresholds and rates are progressive, starting at 1% for incomes over $54,435 and increasing to 10% for incomes over $151,201. HECS/HELP repayments are not a tax but are deducted from your pay via PAYG withholding if you notify your employer.

Superannuation

Superannuation (super) is Australia's compulsory retirement savings system. For 2024–25, employers must contribute 11.5% of an employee's ordinary time earnings into a super fund. This is paid on top of your salary and is not deducted from your pay. The super guarantee rate is legislated to increase to 12% from 1 July 2025. Super contributions are taxed at a concessional rate of 15% within the fund.

Salary Sacrifice

Salary sacrifice (also called salary packaging) allows you to redirect a portion of your pre-tax salary into benefits such as additional super contributions, a novated car lease, or other approved items. Because the sacrificed amount is deducted before income tax is calculated, it can reduce your taxable income and overall tax liability. However, salary sacrifice into super is subject to concessional contribution caps ($30,000 per year for 2024–25, including employer contributions).

Tips to Increase Your Take-Home Pay in Australia

  • Salary sacrifice into super: Contributions up to the concessional cap reduce your taxable income and are taxed at only 15% in the fund.
  • Claim work-related deductions: Expenses like home office costs, uniforms, tools, and travel can reduce your taxable income at tax time.
  • Get private health insurance: If you earn over $93,000, holding hospital cover avoids the Medicare levy surcharge (1%–1.5%).
  • Review your PAYG withholding: If you consistently receive large tax refunds, you may be able to apply for a withholding variation to increase your regular take-home pay.
  • Use the low-income tax offset: If eligible, LITO can reduce your tax payable by up to $700 — it is automatically applied when you lodge your tax return.
