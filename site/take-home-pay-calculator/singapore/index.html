How Singapore Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

Your take-home pay in Singapore is your gross salary minus income tax and your employee Central Provident Fund (CPF) contribution. Unlike many countries, Singapore has no sales tax on income, no capital gains tax, and relatively low personal income tax rates, making it one of the most tax-friendly jurisdictions in the world.

Singapore Income Tax (YA 2024)

Singapore uses a progressive income tax system administered by the Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS). Tax residents are taxed on a tiered bracket system starting at 0% for the first S$20,000 of chargeable income and rising to a top marginal rate of 22% for income above S$320,000. Non-residents are generally taxed at a flat rate of 22% (or the progressive resident rate, whichever gives a higher tax), with some exceptions for short-term employment.

The tax brackets for resident individuals in Year of Assessment 2024 are:

  • First S$20,000: 0%
  • Next S$10,000 (S$20,001 to S$30,000): 2%
  • Next S$10,000 (S$30,001 to S$40,000): 3.5%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$40,001 to S$80,000): 7%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$80,001 to S$120,000): 11.5%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$120,001 to S$160,000): 15%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$160,001 to S$200,000): 18%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$200,001 to S$240,000): 19%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$240,001 to S$280,000): 19.5%
  • Next S$40,000 (S$280,001 to S$320,000): 20%
  • Above S$320,000: 22%

Personal Tax Reliefs

Singapore provides several personal tax reliefs that reduce your chargeable income. The most common is the Earned Income Relief, which is automatically applied: S$1,000 for individuals below age 55, S$6,000 for those aged 55 to 59, and S$8,000 for those aged 60 and above. Other reliefs include course fees relief, life insurance relief, CPF relief (for employee contributions), NS-man relief, parent/handicapped parent relief, spouse relief, qualifying/handicapped child relief, and more. This calculator applies the earned income relief and CPF relief automatically.

Central Provident Fund (CPF)

CPF is Singapore's mandatory social security savings scheme for working Singaporeans and permanent residents. Both employees and employers contribute a percentage of the employee's wages. The employee contribution rate depends on age:

  • Age 55 and below: 20% of ordinary wages
  • Age 56 to 60: 17%
  • Age 61 to 65: 11.5%
  • Age 66 to 70: 7.5%
  • Above age 70: 5%

CPF contributions are capped at the Ordinary Wage (OW) ceiling of S$6,800 per month (S$81,600 per year). Wages above this ceiling do not attract CPF contributions. Employee CPF contributions are tax-deductible, meaning they reduce your chargeable income for income tax purposes.

Non-citizens (including Employment Pass and S Pass holders) are generally not required to contribute to CPF, though first- and second-year permanent residents may have graduated rates. This calculator includes a CPF opt-out checkbox for non-citizens.

Resident vs Non-Resident Tax

You are considered a tax resident of Singapore if you are a Singapore citizen or permanent resident residing in Singapore, or a foreigner who has stayed or worked in Singapore for 183 days or more in a calendar year. Non-residents face a flat 22% tax rate on employment income (or progressive resident rates, whichever is higher), with no personal reliefs. This calculator assumes resident tax status. If you are a non-resident, consult IRAS for your specific tax obligations.

Tips to Optimise Your Take-Home Pay in Singapore

  • Maximise tax reliefs: Claim all eligible reliefs such as course fees (up to S$5,500), NSman relief, parent relief, and working mother's child relief to reduce chargeable income.
  • Voluntary CPF top-ups: Cash top-ups to your Special Account or MediSave (up to S$8,000 per year) qualify for additional tax relief while boosting your retirement savings.
  • SRS contributions: Contributing to a Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS) account gives you dollar-for-dollar tax relief up to S$15,300 per year (S$35,700 for foreigners).
  • Charitable donations: Donations to approved Institutions of a Public Character (IPCs) qualify for 250% tax deduction.
  • Understand your employment benefits: Some benefits like employer CPF contributions are not taxable, while others like housing benefits may be.
