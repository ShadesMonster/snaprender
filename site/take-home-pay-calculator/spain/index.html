How Take-Home Pay Is Calculated in Spain

Your take-home pay in Spain (salario neto) is what remains from your gross salary (salario bruto) after two main deductions: IRPF income tax and social security contributions (cotizaciones a la Seguridad Social). Unlike the US system, Spain does not have a separate state-level income tax return; instead, the regional portion is collected alongside the national IRPF through a single withholding process managed by the Agencia Tributaria.

IRPF (Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas)

Spain's personal income tax, known as IRPF, is a progressive tax applied to your taxable income. The tax is split between the national government and your autonomous community (comunidad autónoma). The national portion ranges from 9.5% to 24.5%, while most regions add a similar schedule, resulting in combined marginal rates from 19% on the first €12,450 up to 47% on income above €300,000. This calculator uses the combined national + average regional rates, which are representative for most of mainland Spain.

Before applying the tax brackets, Spain allows a personal minimum (mínimo personal) of €5,550 per year, which is the amount of income considered necessary for basic living expenses and is effectively tax-free. Additional amounts apply for children: €2,400 for the first child, €2,700 for the second, €4,000 for the third, and €4,500 for each additional child. An employment income reduction (reducción por rendimientos del trabajo) of €2,000 also applies to salaried workers earning below certain thresholds, further reducing your taxable base.

Spanish IRPF Tax Brackets (2024, Combined National + Regional)

  • €0 – €12,450: 19%
  • €12,451 – €20,200: 24%
  • €20,201 – €35,200: 30%
  • €35,201 – €60,000: 37%
  • €60,001 – €300,000: 45%
  • €300,001+: 47%

Social Security Contributions (Seguridad Social)

All employees in Spain contribute to the social security system. The employee's share is approximately 6.35% of gross salary, broken down as follows: 4.7% for general contingencies (contingencias comunes), 1.55% for unemployment (desempleo), and 0.1% for professional training (formación profesional). These contributions are capped at a maximum monthly base of €4,720.50 (approximately €56,646 per year in 2024). Income above this cap is not subject to additional employee social security contributions.

The employer also pays a much larger share (around 30%) on top of your salary, but this does not come out of your paycheck and is not shown in this calculator.

Pagas Extras (14 Payments per Year)

A distinctive feature of Spanish payroll is the system of "pagas extras" (extra payments). Most Spanish employment contracts split the annual salary into 14 payments rather than 12. The two extra payments are traditionally paid in June (paga de verano) and December (paga de Navidad). Your total annual salary remains the same whether paid in 12 or 14 installments — the only difference is the size of each individual payment. This calculator supports both options so you can see your actual monthly take-home under either arrangement.

Regional Tax Variations

While the national IRPF brackets are uniform across Spain, each autonomous community sets its own regional (autonómica) tax schedule. In practice, the regional rates roughly mirror the national portion, giving the combined rates used in this calculator. Some regions like Madrid have slightly lower rates, while Catalonia and Valencia tend to be slightly higher. For most employees, the difference is within 1–2 percentage points of the average combined rates shown here.

Tips to Optimize Your Take-Home Pay in Spain

  • Claim all applicable deductions: Make sure your employer knows about your children and other personal circumstances so your IRPF withholding is calculated correctly from the start.
  • Contribute to a plan de pensiones: Contributions to Spanish pension plans (up to €1,500/year) reduce your IRPF taxable base.
  • Consider cheques restaurante: Meal vouchers up to €11/day are exempt from IRPF, which can boost your effective take-home pay.
  • Check your comunidad autónoma: Regional deductions for rent, childcare, and other expenses can lower your IRPF bill at tax filing time.
  • Review your draft declaration: Each spring, the Agencia Tributaria publishes your borrador (draft tax return). Review it carefully to ensure all deductions and personal circumstances are reflected.
