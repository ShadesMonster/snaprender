How Japanese Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

In Japan, your take-home pay (手取り / tedori) is your gross salary (額面 / gakumen) minus three main categories of deductions: national and local income taxes, and social insurance premiums. Unlike many Western countries, Japan uses a unique "employment income deduction" (給与所得控除) system that provides automatic deductions based on your salary level before calculating taxable income.

Employment Income Deduction (給与所得控除)

Every salaried employee in Japan receives an automatic deduction from their gross salary before income tax is calculated. This is not a tax itself but reduces your taxable income. The deduction is tiered: for salaries up to ¥1,625,000 the minimum deduction is ¥550,000, scaling up through brackets that apply decreasing percentages, and capping at ¥1,950,000 for salaries above ¥8,500,000. This system acknowledges that employees have work-related expenses and provides a standardised deduction without requiring individual documentation.

National Income Tax (所得税)

Japan's national income tax uses a progressive bracket system with seven rates ranging from 5% to 45%. After subtracting the employment income deduction and the basic deduction (基礎控除) of ¥480,000 from your salary, the resulting taxable income is applied to these brackets. Each bracket has a corresponding "quick deduction" amount that simplifies the calculation. For example, income between ¥3,300,001 and ¥6,950,000 is taxed at 20% with a quick deduction of ¥427,500. A reconstruction surtax (復興特別所得税) of 2.1% is added on top of your calculated income tax to fund recovery from the 2011 earthquake, and remains in effect through 2037.

Resident Tax (住民税)

Resident tax (住民税 / juuminzei) is a local tax collected by your municipality and prefecture. It is approximately 10% of your taxable income (typically 6% municipal + 4% prefectural), plus a per-capita levy of about ¥5,000 per year. Unlike income tax which is withheld from your monthly salary in real time, resident tax is based on your previous year's income and is deducted from your salary starting in June. Each dependent reduces your resident tax through a ¥330,000 deduction per dependent from taxable income.

Social Insurance (社会保険)

Social insurance premiums in Japan are shared between employer and employee. As an employee, you pay approximately 14.75% of your salary across three programmes:

  • Health Insurance (健康保険): Approximately 5% of your standard monthly remuneration (標準報酬月額). The exact rate varies by prefecture and health insurance society, but averages around 5% for the employee share. There is a cap at a standard monthly remuneration of ¥1,390,000.
  • Employees' Pension (厚生年金): 9.15% of your standard monthly remuneration, capped at a standard monthly remuneration of ¥650,000 (approximately ¥7,800,000 per year). This is Japan's mandatory corporate pension system.
  • Employment Insurance (雇用保険): 0.6% of your salary with no cap. This covers unemployment benefits and job training.

Social insurance premiums are deducted before income tax is calculated, which provides a tax benefit. Your employer pays an equal or slightly higher share on top of your deductions.

Bonuses in Japan

Many Japanese companies pay bonuses (賞与 / shouyo) twice a year, typically in June and December, on top of 12 monthly salary payments. These bonuses are subject to the same income tax and social insurance deductions. When using this calculator with the "Monthly + Bonus (14 payments)" option, the annual salary is divided into 14 equal portions to approximate the common practice of receiving 12 monthly payments plus two bonus payments.

Tips to Increase Your Take-Home Pay in Japan

  • Claim dependents correctly: Each qualifying dependent reduces your resident tax liability through additional deductions.
  • Use iDeCo (個人型確定拠出年金): Contributions to Japan's individual defined contribution pension plan are fully tax-deductible, reducing both income tax and resident tax.
  • Hometown tax (ふるさと納税): Donate to rural municipalities and receive tax credits plus regional gifts, effectively redirecting resident tax payments.
  • Medical expense deduction: If your household's annual medical expenses exceed ¥100,000, you can claim a deduction via your annual tax return (確定申告).
  • Mortgage deduction: Homeowners can receive a significant income tax credit for up to 13 years on their home loan balance.
